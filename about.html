About Orion

A veteran-owned small business built around contingency response readiness.

Orion Contingency Group Inc. provides contingency response training and assessment, landing zone and airfield assessment, exercise design, curriculum development, and readiness advisory to defense and government organizations.

Company

Who we are.

Orion Contingency Group is headquartered in San Antonio, Texas and operates through three divisions. The Orion Division of Operations delivers landing zone, assault zone, and airfield assessment. Advisory Services delivers exercise design, curriculum development, and independent readiness assessment. The Orion Institute of Training delivers a seven-course contingency response curriculum by mobile training team.

Our cadre are career instructors in air transportation, aerial port, airfield operations, and contingency response. Their credentials are attributable to named individuals and verifiable on request. The company's operating premise follows from that background: readiness is decided by whether a unit can assess, open, and hand over a site to a published standard, and the fastest way to raise it is to train and evaluate on the unit's own ramp, with the unit's own equipment, against the doctrine the unit is inspected to.

OCG trains, documents, and independently assesses. The Government retains qualification, certification, survey approval, and operational authority. OCG grants no credential it is not entitled to grant.

Core competencies

  • Contingency response training and assessment. Operations leadership, skills sustainment, command and control under degraded conditions, and transfer of forces between seizing, contingency response, and follow-on forces.
  • Landing zone and assault zone assessment. A seven-task assessment methodology aligned to DAFMAN 13-217, with helicopter landing zone survey and assessment, delivered inside the contractor provisions the manual defines.
  • Aerial port, ramp, and cargo operations training. Load team development against QTP task numbers, 463L systems, joint inspection, and expeditionary ramp and flow management, delivered on the customer's ramp.
  • Exercise design, control, and evaluation. Scenario, inject, and evaluator packages; structured after-action capture and cross-unit correlation.
  • Curriculum development. Published objective-writing standard, instructor and student guides, practical exercises, assessment instruments, and capstone design.
  • Readiness assessment and advisory. Independent evaluation against published standards, corrective action planning, and tracked adoption.

Differentiators

  • Mobile training team delivery at the customer's location. No student travel, no formal-school seat consumed, customer equipment used so the training is real.
  • Courseware written to published doctrine, cited at paragraph level: AFTTP 3-4.7, DAFMAN 13-217, and AFDN 1-21. Where published guidance states a value, it governs and OCG's interim value is withdrawn.
  • Documented scope limits. OCG trains, documents, and independently assesses. The Government retains qualification, certification, survey approval, and operational authority.
  • Cadre of career instructors in air transportation, aerial port, airfield operations, and contingency response. Credentials are attributable to named individuals and verifiable on request.
  • Owner-funded and debt-free, with disciplined cost control and no legacy overhead.
  • Scheduling built around drill weekends and annual training; completion memos, graded lane sheets, rosters, and progress documentation returned to the customer's training office.
